The Opportunity

The engines are already running.

Browser 4X is the genre where players stay for years, not for a launch window. It is also the genre NOXGAMES has quietly built twice over. Both games are live right now. What they do not have, and what Warhorse can give in a single signature, is a world people already care about.

Two engines, both live

A rendered 3D engine with seasonal worlds, a hex map, settlement building and deterministic replayable battles. And an illustrated 2D engine carrying a full Travian-class economy: 41 buildings, 80 units, six factions, heroes, relics and an endgame race. Both are in production today, not on a roadmap.

Bohemia is already in there

Our 2D engine already ships a Czech Hussite faction: a wagon fort that shrugs off battering rams, a tavern where a feast lifts the defenders' morale, its own units and its own painted village art. The medieval Central European layer exists and is balanced. Kingdom Come gives it a name millions of people already know.

A genre that compounds

4X worlds run in seasons. Every round resets the race but keeps the account, so the game earns across years instead of decaying after launch week. The players generate the drama, which means no content treadmill and no studio hours from your side, ever.

The World

Build it back. Then hold it.

One shared Bohemia, thousands of players, one season. You begin with a burnt clearing and a handful of villagers. What you make of it, and whether it is still standing when Sigismund's army arrives, is the whole game.

Your Pribyslavitz

The same reconstruction the single-player game made famous, turned into the core loop: charcoal burner, smithy, tavern, mill, church, guardhouse, stables. Fields feed queues, queues cost real time and real resources, and a village left undefended is a village someone else farms.

Your retinue

Train, equip and march. Attack, raid, scout, reinforce. Every battle resolves on the server and replays deterministically, so a report is not a number, it is a fight you can watch back round by round and argue about in alliance chat.

Your alliance

Banners of up to thirty. Reinforcements that physically garrison a friend's walls and eat their grain. Shared defence calls, a marketplace, and a chat that runs hot for the whole season. The castles below are not solo content.

Already Built

Not a pitch deck. A running game.

Every system below is written, balanced and in the hands of players in our own titles today. Kingdom Come is the art, the data and the naming on top. That is why this reads in months rather than years, and why the risk sits closer to a re-theme than to a new build.

Economy in real time

Resource fields, build and training queues, warehouse limits, crop upkeep and starvation. The simulation is lazy and time-based, so a world costs almost nothing to run between logins.

Battles you can watch

Combat resolves on the server and replays deterministically from a seed. A report is not a number, it is a fight you can watch back round by round and argue about afterwards.

Alliances that matter

Banners of up to thirty. Reinforcements that physically garrison a friend's walls and eat their grain. Shared defence calls, donations, and a chat that runs hot all season.

Seasons that end

Worlds finalise on a points target or a date, pay out a hall of fame and reset the race while accounts persist. This is what makes the title earn across years instead of weeks.

Heroes and gear

Attributes, levels, five equipment slots across tiers, adventures and expeditions with real choices. The single-player hook that keeps people logging in on quiet days.

Markets and trade

Caravans with real travel time, player-to-player offers held in escrow, and an NPC exchange. Economy players and war players need each other, which is where alliances come from.

Relics and the endgame

Treasuries to raid, relics with permanent account-wide effects, and a great build that ends the round when it tops out. Every season has a finish line worth racing to.

Worlds that fill themselves

NPC settlers with personalities seed a new world and keep growing, building and raiding on their own. Day one never feels empty, which is where most new strategy worlds die.

One pot, one threshold

Your investment is a single number: the development budget plus whatever you spend on user acquisition. The 70/30 split runs until that number is back. Revenue means net receipts after payment processing, and nothing else is deducted, so you can audit the whole deal on one line.

Our delivery guarantee

If NOXGAMES does not deliver the agreed scope at the agreed date, the delivery milestone is dropped in full. You keep that third and the world still opens. We carry the schedule risk, in writing.

What you never provide

People, and running costs. No developers, artists, producers, community managers or live-ops staff, and no hosting bill: we carry it out of our own share. Alongside the budget, Warhorse commits a minimum of €500K to UA in the first 3 months after launch. If that commitment is not met, NOXGAMES receives one third of the development cost as reparation, mirroring our delivery guarantee.

IP & Assets

Your world, handled with care.

Nothing ships without your approval, and everything we produce from your source material stays yours to use. A persistent world writes its own stories, so the canon rules matter more here than in a single-player-shaped product, and we treat them that way.

You share sources and canon

Warhorse provides source materials and style guides: characters, environments, heraldry, place names, references and the canon rules that come with them.

We build and run the world

NOXGAMES turns them into a living game: regions, castles, units, buildings, heroes and UI. Every asset, every unit name and every event passes your brand and canon review before it goes live.

Assets flow back

Upon request, everything we create is delivered back to Warhorse for further use in your own games and campaigns. A marketing license for these assets is available as an add-on.
